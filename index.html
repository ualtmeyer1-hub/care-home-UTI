When to Suspect a UTI & Collect a Sample

DO NOT DIPSTICK URINE in Residents (65+) or Catheters

Up to 50% of care home residents and nearly 100% of long-term catheters have harmless bacteria in their urine. Dipsticks almost always turn positive, causing false alarms and unnecessary antibiotics.

    How to Collect the Urine Sample Properly

    UK SMI B 41
    1
    Collect BEFORE starting antibiotics
    Unless a GP/paramedic says urgent sepsis treatment cannot wait.
    2
    Clean-catch / Midstream Urine (MSU) only
    Clean genital area with water. Catch midstream urine in a sterile receiver. NEVER squeeze urine from incontinence pads or collect from a non-sterile bedpan/commode.
    3
    Fill Red-Top Boric Acid tube strictly to the 10 mL line
    Underfilling makes boric acid too concentrated, which kills the bacteria and causes a false-negative result. (If <5 mL, use a plain white-top pot and refrigerate at 2–8°C).
    NEVER take urine from the drainage bag! Bag urine contains old bacteria and gives a false result.
    1
    Catheter in place > 7 days: Change catheter first
    Arrange for a trained nurse to replace the old catheter BEFORE collecting the sample and starting antibiotics.
    2
    Use the Needleless Sampling Port
    Briefly clamp tubing below port until fresh urine is visible. Wipe port with a 70% alcohol wipe and let dry for 15 seconds. Aspirate with a sterile syringe.
    3
    Fill Red-Top Boric Acid tube strictly to the 10 mL line
    Unclamp tubing immediately after sampling and label clearly as “CSU — Catheter Specimen of Urine”.
    1. Ban on Urine Dipsticks in 65+ & Catheters (UKHSA Grade A | SIGN 160 1++): 25–50% of care home residents and ~100% of long-term catheters have asymptomatic bacteriuria. Dipsticking drives false-positive diagnoses, unnecessary antibiotics, and C. difficile infection.
    2. Symptom Thresholds (UKHSA Grade B | SIGN 160 2+): New dysuria alone, ≥2 localised urinary symptoms, or systemic pyelonephritis/sepsis signs predict true UTI. Confusion or dark/smelly urine alone require PINCH ME & hydration assessment first.
    3. Specimen Collection (UK SMI B 41 Issue 8.8): Pre-antibiotic clean-catch MSU or aseptic catheter sampling port aspiration (never bag/pad), catheter change if in situ >7 days, and filling Red-Top Boric Acid containers to the 10 mL line.
