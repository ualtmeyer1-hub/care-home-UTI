Understanding Your Urine Test

Getting the Right Care for Urine Infections (UTIs) — A Guide for Patients and Families

When you or a loved one feels unwell, it’s natural to want answers and the right treatment. This guide explains how we decide if a urine test (called a urine culture) is needed to help us treat a “waterworks” infection (Urinary Tract Infection or UTI), and why making the right test choice is so important.

A UTI happens when germs that normally live in the gut get into the bladder or kidneys and cause illness. Common signs of a UTI can include:

  • Pain or burning when you pee
  • Needing to pee very often, but only a little bit comes out at a time
  • A sudden, strong urge to pee that cannot be put off
  • New or worsening leaking (incontinence)
  • Pain in your lower tummy or back
Sometimes, a UTI can make people feel very unwell, with a fever and shivering. When this happens, confusion is a common additional feature.

A urine culture is a lab test that looks for the germs in your pee that have caused your infection. If germs grow in the lab, they are tested against different antibiotic medicines to help us know which one will work best to treat them.

This might sound surprising, but sometimes a urine test isn’t the best first step. Here’s why:

“Germs Without Symptoms” (Asymptomatic Bacteriuria – ASB)

It’s very common for some people, especially older adults or those with a catheter (a tube in the bladder to drain pee), to have germs in their pee without actually having an infection. This is called ASB and is harmless.

Treating these “germs without symptoms” doesn’t make them go away and can cause harm. It can lead to needless side effects from antibiotics and will make antibiotic medicines less effective for future infections.

Misleading Signs

  • Dipstick tests (quick tests done with a strip) can often show “positive” results even when there’s no infection. They can be misleading.
  • Pee that looks dark or cloudy or smells strongly is usually a sign of not drinking enough fluids, not an infection.
  • New confusion, agitation, or a recent fall: On their own, these are very common in older adults and are usually caused by dehydration, constipation, pain, or medication — not a urine infection — unless accompanied by bladder pain or a high temperature.

Unnecessary Risks

  • Giving antibiotics when they’re not really needed can cause side effects like sickness, thrush, rashes, or even serious gut infections (like “C. diff”) without you getting any benefits from the treatment.
  • It also contributes to “superbugs” – germs that have become resistant to many antibiotic types, making real infections much harder to treat in the future.

We prioritise urine tests when they will truly help guide treatment and keep you safe. We will usually send a urine test if you have:

  • Two or more new or worsening symptoms of a urine infection (like pain when peeing, rushing, or new leaking).
  • Serious signs of infection, such as a fever, shivering, or if you are very unwell (signs of sepsis).
  • Specific health conditions that make UTIs more serious, such as pregnancy, a recent kidney transplant, or if you have a catheter and new signs of being unwell.
  • Symptoms that don’t improve with initial treatment.

If a urine culture is useful, getting a good sample is important to avoid misleading results. We will give you clear instructions, but generally:

1
Mid-stream sample: We’ll ask you to collect a “mid-stream” sample (peeing a little into the toilet bowl first, then into the sample pot). There’s a leaflet with instructions you can ask for that explains exactly how to do this properly.
2
If you have a catheter: We’ll take the sample from the catheter itself, not the bag. If your catheter has been in for a while we’ll change the catheter first.
3
Special red-top pot: We’ll use a special red-top pot (filled to the line so the preservative keeps the sample accurate).
4
Tell us your symptoms: It’s vital that we know your symptoms. Please tell your healthcare team exactly what and how you’re feeling.

Our Commitment to Your Safety

Your healthcare team is making careful decisions about your treatment. By only sending urine tests when they are truly needed, we are:

  • Protecting you from unnecessary antibiotics and their side effects.
  • Making sure antibiotics work when you really need them.
  • Saving valuable time for you and our staff, so they can focus on what matters most.

If you have any questions or concerns, please don’t hesitate to ask your doctor or nurse.
